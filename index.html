Edge-first: the device does the cheap 99%

🎧 Listen

On-device audio onset picker hears the bat crack on the continuous stream and segments the ball — no video ever uploaded.

👁️ Track

A $0 motion-blob tracker finds the 3-pixel ball SAM can't, confirms contact, and picks exactly two stills: contact + outcome.

⚖️ Judge

Only those 2 JPEGs (~60KB) leave the device: SAM 3.1 checks the stumps, Muse Spark calls the ball. ~$0.0029, ~6s, proven 6/6 end-to-end from edge-selected stills.

Three surfaces, one umpire

Frontier · DAT-ready

🕶️ On the lens

Glasses stream the umpire's view and the mid-pitch sound; the verdict returns as a card on the lens and a word in the ear. Confirm or correct by voice — the umpire never touches a screen. Capture plugs into the same CaptureSource interface via Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit.

Working today

📱 On any phone

The verified core: SAM 3.1 checks the stumps, a $0 motion tracker finds a 3-pixel ball, Muse Spark calls runs/boundary/wicket as JSON, MCC geometry arbitrates where the angle is calibrated. One command replays a whole over.

Working today

🧑‍⚖️ In the scorebook

Every ball lands in an annotation dashboard: timeline, running score, watch-links. Any verdict can be corrected; corrections export as ground truth. AI calls it, humans confirm it, every match makes the next call better.

Why glasses change the game

Best seat, every ball

The umpire's eye line is the one camera angle cricket is actually judged from — no tripod, no cameraman, no missed delivery.

Sound where it happens

A mid-pitch microphone hears the snick a boundary phone never will. Appeals, edges, and bat-cracks arrive with the picture.

Hands stay free

Verdict in the ear, card on the lens, confirm by voice. The human remains the umpire — the AI is the assistant that never blinks.
