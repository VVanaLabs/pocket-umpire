Why it works now — edge first

Listen on-device

An audio onset picker segments every ball locally. No video ever leaves the device.

Track on-device

A $0 motion-blob tracker finds the 3-pixel ball SAM can't, and picks two stills: contact + outcome (~60KB).

Judge in the cloud

Only those stills go up: SAM 3.1 + Muse Spark return a verdict in ~6s — inside the natural gap between balls, for $0.0029.

The market

Every weekend

grassroots cricket matches are played across South Asia, the UK, Australia, and the global diaspora — tape-ball, club, school, and village cricket, almost none of it officiated.

~$0 today

is spent officiating almost all of them. There is no incumbent: the alternative is a player-umpire and an argument.

1 phone

is the entire setup. Every team already has the hardware in someone's pocket — distribution is a link, not a sale of equipment.

Wedge: tape-ball and club leagues that already film matches for social media. They have the video, the audience, and the arguments — we give them the umpire and the highlights.

Business model

$2–5 / match for live scoring + verdicts. Compute costs us under $1* at contributor pricing ($1.86 at Standard pricing).

League season

Per-team subscription: fixtures, scorebooks, player stats, and season highlights — the record grassroots cricket never had.

Highlights

Grassroots cricket has no labeled dataset — it has never been filmed systematically. Every corrected ball in our dashboard is a training example nobody else has, across exactly the conditions (night tape-ball, village whites, board stumps) that broadcast-trained models have never seen.

Why now

Models crossed the line

SAM 3.1 segments a wicket from one still in 0.9s; Muse Spark judges outcomes from two frames for fractions of a cent. This was impossible at any price two years ago.

Glasses are the endgame

The umpire's eye line is the perfect camera and the mid-pitch mic hears the snick. Meta's Wearables DAT makes that stream programmable today.

The footage already exists

Grassroots matches are already filmed for YouTube and WhatsApp. We don't change behavior — we officiate it.
